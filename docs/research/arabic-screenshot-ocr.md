# Arabic screenshot OCR: model and pipeline research

Researched 2026-11-05. Scope: lightweight, local Arabic screenshot OCR for the existing Rust/ONNX worker. Sources are project code, official documentation, model-author material, and the original issue report; model-card and issue results are author-reported, not independent benchmarks.

## Decision and current evidence

Keep the existing official Arabic PP-OCRv5 mobile recognizer and improve its line preprocessing in the native worker. PaddleOCR's current [language-to-model selector](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/blob/main/paddleocr/_pipelines/ocr.py) chooses PP-OCRv5 for Arabic languages and constructs `arabic_PP-OCRv5_mobile_rec`; its PP-OCRv6 selection covers its enumerated languages instead. The project's [release overview](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR) describes the unified v6 model as Chinese, English, Japanese and 46 Latin-script languages. This is a statement about the official lightweight recognition families and current selector, not a claim that no Arabic model exists anywhere.

The existing `paddle-ar-en` catalog entry pairs the Arabic v5 recognizer and dictionary with a v5 detector (`assets/catalog/modules.json`). On the user's 868×313 screenshot, the project investigation reproduced corrupted recognition **before** text cleanup or rendering, including omitted numbers. An adaptive experiment using native and bounded roughly 2× horizontal crop widths improved that exact image. This is a single observed case, not a measured general accuracy guarantee. Preserve native results for Latin lines and use Arabic detection and recognition confidence when considering the wider candidate; confidence is an imperfect proxy and can be wrong. Evaluate Arabic, English, mixed-script text, numbers, punctuation, diacritics and short/long lines before generalizing. Avoid logging private screenshot text during routine operation.

[PaddleOCR issue #18349](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/issues/18349) reports similar failure of the Arabic v5 recognizer on dense full-width lines. Its author reports lower character error rates on two scanned-book samples using per-crop `clamp(round(2 × 48 × width / height), 320, 1280)` instead of natural aspect-ratio width. The issue explicitly cautions that the evidence does not isolate CTC sequence capacity from resampling, and that a global policy needs cross-model and cross-language evaluation. Its reported numbers are not measurements of our screenshot or this implementation.

In the installed OAR OCR 0.10.2 source, recognition preprocess normally scales crops to height 48, chooses a batch width from aspect ratio with a 320-pixel minimum, and clamps at 3,200 pixels. Crops only become horizontally compressed by the cap when their aspect ratio exceeds about 66.7; poor recognition below that threshold is still possible. The public high-level builder does not expose a per-crop width multiplier. Its lower-level `max_img_w` option is not wired through the CRNN builder in this version. The experiment therefore uses the public detection and recognition adapters and per-line crops, without a dependency fork. These are observations from the installed crate source, not a cross-version API promise.

## Other open-source implementations

- [RapidOCR's pipeline](https://github.com/RapidAI/RapidOCR/blob/main/python/rapidocr/main.py) bounds image dimensions, detects text boxes, produces rotated perspective crops, and recognizes each crop; optional orientation classification is a separate stage. This supports retaining a detector/crop/recognizer separation but does not itself demonstrate better recognition on our image. Its recognition wrapper also applies `reorder_bidi_for_display` for RTL languages after recognition; that is separate from raw decoding, so avoid applying display reordering twice or passing display-shaped text to speech. The inspected [RapidOCR CTC decoder](https://github.com/RapidAI/RapidOCR/blob/main/python/rapidocr/ch_ppocr_rec/utils.py) does not expose the Arabic-specific reversal found in [PaddleOCR's decoder](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/blob/main/ppocr/postprocess/rec_postprocess.py), which groups ASCII/digit runs before reversing Arabic-class output. Do not assume changing wrappers automatically fixes visual-versus-logical ordering or mixed-script punctuation.
- [Umi-OCR](https://github.com/hiroi-sora/Umi-OCR) is an offline screenshot and document OCR application. Its documented architecture uses a Python/Qt application with separate PaddleOCR-json or RapidOCR-json engines. It is a useful workflow reference for screenshot selection and presentation, but its application layer is not evidence of a different Arabic recognizer or superior output on this image. Integrating the whole application would add a separate application/runtime instead of addressing the observed worker behavior.

## Community Arabic PP-OCRv6

[medyas/arabic_PP-OCRv6_small_rec](https://huggingface.co/medyas/arabic_PP-OCRv6_small_rec) is a community fine-tune of PP-OCRv6 small for single-line printed Arabic, not an official Paddle Arabic release. The author reports 13.5% strict CER on 500 held-out presentation/document crops and 50.3% on 200 historical scanned crops; the 10.2% scan result is from the checkpoint-selection set. These are the author's measurements and are not directly comparable to our one screenshot. The model card also says its v6 and v5 training corpora differ, so that comparison does not isolate model architecture. Its visual-order output needs correct Arabic/digit reordering; English recognition quality is unknown.

At the inspected [pinned revision](https://huggingface.co/medyas/arabic_PP-OCRv6_small_rec/tree/0f1155db29389258f18e9232a770859719d3c6e9), the published artifacts include Paddle training weights and a [PaddleInference export](https://huggingface.co/medyas/arabic_PP-OCRv6_small_rec/tree/0f1155db29389258f18e9232a770859719d3c6e9/inference), with no ONNX artifact listed. It is not a drop-in replacement for the current ONNX worker. Reconsider only after a reproducible conversion, license and provenance review, compatibility and memory checks, and independent Arabic/English/mixed-script evaluation on representative captures.

## Practical scope

The width adjustment and Arabic decoding can run in the existing Rust worker and current ONNX Runtime. They do not require adding Python, another OCR runtime, an external OCR application, an LLM, or network access. Keep the original model and ordinary preprocessing for other model profiles. Record before/after comparisons at the recognition stage and check complete text—including digits and Latin runs—rather than judging only visual layout. The research pass itself did not run tests or new experiments.

## Local comparison (2026-11-05)

The user-provided Wikipedia crop reproduced corruption in the OCR worker's raw Unicode output, before speech cleanup or tooltip rendering. The original worker dropped complete letter sequences and the numbers 467 and 27. The existing renderer connects Arabic letters correctly; it cannot recover missing recognition output.

The comparison used the same official Arabic v5 ONNX weights with OAR and RapidOCR 3.10.0. RapidOCR used its Arabic language mode, six-crop recognition batches, the v5 detector's required ImageNet normalization, and its normal detector settings. Both sessions reported CUDA execution. The older official Arabic v3 recognizer was also evaluated; no other OCR catalog models were tested.

The generated corpus has 72 condition runs and 50 distinct images. It varies Arabic fonts (Noto Sans Arabic and Noto Naskh Arabic), sizes 16/22/32, light/dark/low-contrast blue themes, short and long lines, paragraphs, English, and mixed Latin/digits. DejaVu Sans supplies the mixed-script cases because the bundled Arabic font does not contain Latin letters. Fontconfig character coverage and visual inspection exclude missing-glyph fixtures. Duplicate mixed-script images across the two font passes are retained in the condition-run averages; these are synthetic regression checks, **not estimates of general OCR accuracy**.

| Pipeline | Mean character error | Median time after model load per generated image |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Existing OAR + Arabic v5 | 16.20% | 0.26 s |
| OAR + older Arabic v3 | 23.66% | 0.26 s |
| OAR with Rapid-style detector settings | 65.02% | not compared (development build) |
| RapidOCR + same Arabic v5 weights | 67.41% | 0.35 s |
| Packaged OAR with guarded, batched alternate sizing | 0.28% | 0.44 s |

These timings include shape changes between generated images; only the repeated-paragraph figures reuse the exact same image, and neither should be treated as a hardware benchmark. Character error uses Unicode decomposition, removes combining marks and punctuation/spacing, and folds Arabic alef maksura to ya. A second metric retains normalized word boundaries; the final approach scores 0.36% there. It improves 32 condition runs, matches 39, and worsens one short Naskh case by one character. All ordinary English worker fixtures also pass. This limited corpus does not cover handwriting, photographs, arbitrary layouts, or all screen fonts. RapidOCR's fragment ordering and different detector behavior contribute to its result; this is a comparison of these configured pipelines, not a claim that its recognition library is generally inferior.

The final code retains OAR's detector, perspective cropper, CTC decoder, normalization and sessions. For the default Arabic v5 profile only, uncertain, elongated Arabic lines receive one bounded alternate width, and that result is accepted only when recognition confidence improves. At most six crops are processed together; alternate inputs are capped at 3200×48. Latin lines keep ordinary sizing. A 0.5 final confidence threshold, as in RapidOCR's defaults, rejects unreliable decoration. There is no contrast-specific, font-specific, Wikipedia-specific, or image-specific rule, new inference engine, Python runtime, or added idle process.

The final Fedora RPM was extracted and tested independently, with the same 0.28% character-error result across the corpus. Median varied-image latency after model load was 0.44 s. The development build's earlier 0.54 s corpus and 2.5 s paragraph measurements are not release-performance comparisons. On the supplied eight-line paragraph, repeated warm GPU calls to the old and new optimized packages take about 0.5 s and 1.3 s respectively. This is an explicit accuracy/latency tradeoff, not a speed improvement. The first serial development implementation took about 4.7 s and was replaced by bounded batches. Occasional spelling, vowel-mark, punctuation and reference errors remain. Complete private inputs, outputs, timings and screenshots are retained under ignored `artifacts/arabic-ocr-regression/`.
